W3NODE HACKATHON — CODEX PROJECT ANALYSIS REQUIREMENTS Track: Merchant &
Machine Payments Document type: Product Requirement / Analysis
Instruction Purpose: Read-only repository, project brief and product
assessment

1.  ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

Act as a combination of: - Senior Software Engineer experienced in
production-grade applications. - Technical Architect specialising in
Web3, blockchain payments, stablecoins, payment infrastructure and
distributed systems. - Multi-time hackathon-winning technical lead who
can turn ambitious ideas into working MVPs under severe time
constraints. - Product strategist who understands customer problems,
technical feasibility, commercial viability and judging criteria.

You are advising our team participating in the W3Node Node Engine
Hackathon 2026, under the Merchant & Machine Payments track.

Your responsibility is to understand what our team is building, assess
our current progress, identify weaknesses and opportunities, and guide
us towards a technically sound, commercially credible and competitive
hackathon submission.

Be direct, analytical, critical and practical. Do not simply agree with
the team or praise the idea.

This document defines an analysis task. Do not implement changes unless
separately instructed and approved.

2.  PHASE 1 — UNDERSTAND THE PROJECT ENVIRONMENT

Before making recommendations, thoroughly inspect the current workspace
and Git repository. Do not modify anything during this initial
assessment.

2.1 Repository inspection

Inspect: - Complete project directory structure. - Git repository status
and current branch. - Existing commits and recent development history. -
README and project documentation. - Frontend architecture. - Backend
architecture. - Database schema and migrations. - API endpoints and
integrations. - Dependencies and package configuration. - Environment
variable configuration. Never expose secret values. - Authentication and
authorisation. - Blockchain integrations, wallets and smart contracts,
if present. - Existing tests and test coverage. - Deployment
configuration. - TODOs, incomplete features and technical debt.

Identify which parts are implemented, partially implemented, planned or
missing.

Do not assume something works simply because its files exist. Trace the
implementation and inspect the actual code. Where appropriate, follow
important user journeys through the frontend, backend, database and
external services.

2.2 Project documentation

Locate and read the following documents wherever they exist in the
repository or accessible workspace: -
Merchant-Machine-Payments-Workshop-Slides.pdf -
START-HERE-Merchant-Machine-Payments-Resources-1.pdf -
team-status-update.md - project-concept-doc.md

Also inspect other relevant project documentation.

If a document is unavailable, explicitly identify it as missing. Do not
pretend to have read it.

Official reference: https://w3node.io/node-engine

Use official W3Node materials as the primary source of hackathon
requirements. If additional research is necessary, distinguish official
requirements from external research and your own recommendations.

2.3 Establish the truth about the project

Determine: - What problem are we solving? - Who experiences this
problem? - Who is our intended customer? - What is our proposed
solution? - How does the proposed product work? - What makes our
solution different? - What technologies are we using? - What has already
been implemented? - What is still being planned? - What assumptions have
not been validated?

Pay particular attention to alignment between our project concept and
the Merchant & Machine Payments track.

Do not substitute your own project idea for ours. Understand our
existing idea first.

3.  PHASE 2 — ASSESS ALIGNMENT WITH W3NODE

Compare the actual project against the official hackathon brief.

3.1 Merchant payments

Assess relevance and implementation of: - Stablecoin acceptance. -
Merchant onboarding and usability. - QR code, NFC or payment-link
functionality, where applicable. - Payment request generation. -
Exchange-rate handling. - Payment verification. - Automatic transaction
matching. - Merchant settlement and cash-out. - Refunds and transaction
history.

3.2 Machine payments

Where relevant to our idea, assess: - Machine-to-machine payment
flows. - HTTP 402 and x402. - Machine Payments Protocol (MPP). -
Automated payment authorisation. - Spending limits and human controls. -
Payment verification before service delivery. - Repeated or metered
transactions.

Do not recommend implementing every technology simply because it appears
in the brief. Determine which technologies are relevant to our proposed
solution.

3.3 Judging requirements

Evaluate the project against the three judging categories: - Product —
30 points. - Technical — 30 points. - Business — 30 points.

Pay particular attention to: - A working end-to-end payment. - At least
one genuine onchain transaction on an appropriate public testnet or
mainnet. - Automatic payment matching. - A usable merchant/operator
dashboard. - Machine spending limits, where applicable. - A credible
customer and business model. - Clear transaction costs and settlement
strategy. - A convincing demonstration.

For each requirement, classify it as: - Satisfied. - Partially
satisfied. - Not implemented. - Not applicable.

Explain the evidence behind each classification. Never award points
based on planned functionality.

4.  PHASE 3 — TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE REVIEW

Act as a senior software architect and critically assess the existing
architecture.

Review: 1. Frontend structure and component design. 2. Backend
architecture and separation of responsibilities. 3. API design. 4.
Database structure and relationships. 5. Blockchain integration. 6.
Payment lifecycle. 7. Wallet integration. 8. Authentication and
security. 9. Error handling. 10. Transaction consistency and
reliability. 11. Deployment and environment configuration. 12. Testing
strategy.

For payment-related functionality, investigate: - How payment requests
are generated. - How transactions are identified. - How blockchain
transactions are verified. - How payments are associated with orders or
sessions. - How duplicate transaction processing is prevented. - How
underpayments and overpayments are handled. - How expired payment
requests are handled. - How refunds are managed. - How payment failures
are communicated. - How goods or services are prevented from being
released before payment verification.

Consider security risks, including: - Private key exposure. -
Unauthorised transactions. - Replay attacks. - Duplicate payment
processing. - Incorrect transaction matching. - Manipulated payment
notifications. - Insufficient spending controls. - Insecure environment
variables.

Do not overengineer the application. Recommend the simplest architecture
that can reliably demonstrate the product.

If current technology choices are appropriate, retain them. Do not
suggest changing frameworks or rewriting working functionality without a
compelling reason.

5.  PHASE 4 — EVALUATE THE IDEA LIKE A HACKATHON WINNER

Imagine evaluating this project against other strong teams competing at
W3Node.

Answer honestly: 1. Is the idea solving a genuine problem? 2. Is the
problem significant enough to justify a product? 3. Is the solution
technically feasible within the hackathon? 4. Is the idea sufficiently
differentiated? 5. Does it make meaningful use of Web3? 6. Does
blockchain genuinely improve the solution compared with traditional
payment infrastructure? 7. Is there a clear customer willing to use it?
8. Can its value be demonstrated in under three minutes? 9. What would
make judges remember it? 10. What could cause the team to lose?

Identify: - Strongest competitive advantages. - Biggest technical
weaknesses. - Biggest business weaknesses. - Unvalidated assumptions. -
Unnecessary complexity. - Features that do not contribute to the MVP. -
Potential differentiators that could materially improve the submission.

Do not recommend adding AI, blockchain features or integrations merely
to make the product appear innovative. Every feature must solve a real
problem.

Be willing to say if the idea is weak, too ambitious or poorly aligned
with the track.

If a better direction exists, explain why, but do not silently replace
the existing concept.

6.  PHASE 5 — DETERMINE WHAT EXISTS SO FAR

Produce a detailed progress report.

6.1 Executive summary

Explain in plain language: - What the project is. - Why it is being
built. - Who it serves. - How it works. - Its current stage.

A person unfamiliar with the project should understand it after reading
this section.

6.2 Current implementation inventory

Create a table with these columns:

Feature | Status | Evidence in repository | Assessment |

Use statuses: Implemented, Partial, Planned or Missing.

Include important frontend, backend, database, blockchain and
infrastructure components.

6.3 Architecture explanation

Explain how the application currently works: - Main components. - Data
flow. - API communication. - Database interactions. - Blockchain
interactions. - Payment lifecycle.

Where useful, provide a Mermaid architecture diagram based on actual
implementation. Do not draw hypothetical components as though they
already exist.

6.4 What has been done well

Identify good engineering and product decisions and explain why they are
good.

6.5 What needs improvement

Identify technical problems, architectural weaknesses, missing
functionality and product concerns. Prioritise issues by actual impact.

7.  PHASE 6 — CREATE THE DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP

Based on the actual codebase, project idea, available time and judging
criteria, develop a realistic roadmap specifically for this W3Node
submission.

Organise recommendations into:

Priority 0 — Critical: Things that must work for a successful MVP.

Priority 1 — Important: Things that significantly improve the product
and judging potential.

Priority 2 — Competitive advantages: Features that could distinguish the
submission if time allows.

Priority 3 — Post-hackathon: Features that should not distract from
delivering the hackathon MVP.

For every task, provide:

Task | Why it matters | Dependencies | Complexity | Priority |
Definition of done |

Use complexity estimates: Small, Medium or Large.

Identify tasks that can be developed independently and tasks that depend
on other components. Recommend a logical implementation order.

Do not create a generic software development roadmap. Base it on the
actual project.

8.  PHASE 7 — PAYMENT DEMONSTRATION STRATEGY

Design the ideal end-to-end demonstration for the specific product.

Explain: 1. Who initiates the payment? 2. What triggers the payment
request? 3. What does the payer see? 4. How is payment authorised? 5.
What happens on the blockchain? 6. How does the backend verify the
transaction? 7. How is the payment matched? 8. What happens after
confirmation? 9. What does the merchant or machine see? 10. How do we
prove that the transaction genuinely happened?

Identify every component needed to make this demonstration reliable.

Recommend a three-minute demonstration structure that communicates the
problem, solution, technical execution and business value.

A genuine working transaction is more important than a polished but
non-functional interface.

9.  PHASE 8 — SENIOR ENGINEER RECOMMENDATIONS

Give practical advice from the perspective of a senior engineer
mentoring a hackathon team.

9.1 Engineering discipline - What should we avoid building? - What
should we simplify? - Where should engineering time be invested? - What
should be tested first? - What architectural mistakes should be
avoided? - Which technical decisions should be made early?

9.2 Product strategy - How should we narrow the MVP? - What should we
demonstrate? - What customer evidence is needed? - How do we make the
product commercially credible?

9.3 Hackathon strategy - What makes a strong submission? - What mistakes
do inexperienced teams make? - How can we maximise our score across
Product, Technical and Business? - How should we communicate
differentiation? - What difficult judge questions should we prepare for?

9.4 Technical learning Identify concepts and technologies the team needs
to understand to build this product successfully. Explain unfamiliar
concepts accessibly, with technical depth where appropriate.

Give the five most important immediate actions in priority order.

Be specific. Reference actual files, functions, components and missing

Leave the team with a clear understanding of: - Exactly where the
